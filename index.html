INVESTIGATIVE CLOSING STATEMENT

The Definitive Theory on the Göhrde Murders

The evaluation of forensic findings and crime scene mapping confirms that Kurt Werner Wichmann acted with cold precision. He chose the dense Göhrde state forest because his background as a local cemetery gardener provided him with impeccable geographical knowledge. This familiarity enabled him to navigate hidden paths and move completely undetected.

The chronological overlap on July 27 is the most critical element of the case. While police investigators actively secured the location of the first victims, Wichmann was already hunting his next targets just eight hundred meters away. This proximity establishes an extreme level of psychopathy and criminal confidence. He utilized a small caliber firearm and specialized knots to bind his victims quickly. This meticulous sequence reveals an experienced perpetrator who maintained total situational control despite the immediate presence of law enforcement officers.

The execution of these simultaneous tasks suggests that a secondary helper was likely involved. Managing the structural logistics of tracking, capturing, and neutralizing two adults at the same time without triggering immediate panic is highly complex. An accomplice could have monitored police movements or helped transport the stolen vehicles away from the forest perimeter.

The current consensus confirms that Wichmann was the primary executioner. The 2017 modern DNA extraction from the hijacked vehicle provides undeniable biological proof. He successfully eluded justice by committing suicide in 1993, but the technological progression of forensic science eventually exposed his ultimate guilt.
